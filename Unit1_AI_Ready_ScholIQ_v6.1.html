Why this unit?
Students arrive with wildly different mental models of AI. This unit establishes the correct model before any tool is introduced: AI as a powerful pattern-matcher that generates likely text, not a thinker that knows truth.

Opening Scenario — Hook
Ahmad came home and discovered he had a science exam the following morning. He had not studied anything, and the required chapter was 25 pages long. He picked up his phone and asked an AI assistant: "Summarize the chapter for me."
Discussion Questions

Click each question to reveal a thinking prompt.

  • 1
    Did Ahmad act wisely?
    Think about the difference between having information and actually understanding it.
  • 2
    Will he actually learn from the summary?
    Learning requires effort. Passively reading a summary is not the same as active study.
  • 3
    What if some of the information is inaccurate?
    AI can generate confident-sounding text that is factually wrong. Without prior knowledge, you can't spot the error.
  • 4
    What if he asked the AI to complete his entire homework?
    There's a spectrum from 'AI helps me learn' to 'AI learns instead of me.' Where is the line?
Watch For…
Read the scenario aloud clearly — or ask a student to read it. Pose the four questions in order. Do not answer any of them; record responses on the board only. 5 minutes is enough. Many students will defend Ahmad immediately — let the discussion evolve.
What Is Artificial Intelligence?
In simple termsAI is a program that learned from massive amounts of text and data, becoming capable of generating answers, writing text, and solving problems.
🤖
Learns from data
Trained on billions of sentences and texts from across the internet and books.
💬
Produces — doesn't find
Creates a brand-new answer each time. It doesn't retrieve from a database — it generates.
⚠️
Doesn't understand — predicts
Chooses what is 'probable' — not what is necessarily correct. That's a critical distinction.
Watch For…
Explain with a simple analogy: 'Imagine it read a billion sentences and then learned which word comes after the sun. That is how it answers.' Pause after the third card and ask: 'Does AI actually understand the meaning?'
AI vs. Search Engine — What's the Difference?
🔍 Search Engine
Finds existing pages on the internet
Displays links to real sources
Results are documented and verifiable
Does NOT create new content
🤖 Artificial Intelligence
Creates a new answer each time
Does not necessarily provide a source
Can be wrong confidently (Hallucination)
Sounds convincing even when incorrect
Key phrase to rememberGoogle FINDS — AI CREATES. One retrieves; the other generates.
Watch For…
Use one live example only: type 'What is light?' in Google, then in an AI tool, and show the difference on screen. One example is enough to clarify the distinction permanently.

⚠ Important Warning
Hallucination
AI sometimes fabricates information that appears correct… but isn't.
Real Example
A student asked AI about the date of a historical battle. AI answered with an incorrect date — but in a confident, convincing academic style. The student submitted the answer on their exam and lost the mark.
Always verify from other reliable sources
Never submit AI's answer directly as your own work
Treat AI as a starting point, not an endpoint
Why This Matters
This is one of the most important slides. Ask students: 'Has something like this happened to you?' Wait for a story from the class. That moment will cement the concept more than any explanation. The term 'Hallucination' will appear in every upcoming unit.

Artificial Intelligence
الذكاء الاصطناعي
Programs that simulate human thinking abilities — learning, reasoning, problem-solving, and generating language.

Generative AI
الذكاء الاصطناعي التوليدي
A type of AI that produces new texts, images, audio, and code — not retrieved, but generated fresh each time.
Tap to reveal ↓
Prompt
الطلب الموجّه
The question or instruction you give to AI. A well-crafted prompt leads to a much better response — prompt quality is thinking quality.
Tap to reveal ↓
Chatbot
روبوت المحادثة
A program that simulates human conversation. Modern AI chatbots use large language models to generate responses.
Tap to reveal ↓
LLM — Large Language Model
النموذج اللغوي الكبير
The most common type of generative AI. Trained on vast amounts of text to predict and generate language at massive scale.
Tap to reveal ↓
Hallucination ⚠
المعلومة المُخترَعة
When AI confidently creates incorrect information — inventing facts, dates, names, or sources that don't exist. This is why verification is always essential.

Lesson Flow Notes
Timing and facilitation tips — aligned to the 15-slide Package C sequence.
S02 · 0–5 min · Essential Question
Display for a full silent minute. Record 3–4 student answers on the board without judging. This question anchors the whole session and returns at S15.

Differentiation by Cognitive Level
Not differentiation by depth only — differentiation by thinking operation. Each grade band targets a different cognitive act.

Activity 1: Students write one question only — they identify which tool gave them links vs. a written answer.

Grades 9–10

Bloom: Apply & Analyze
⚖️ COMPARE

🗣 JUSTIFY

S13: After choosing an equation, students must justify: "I chose this because…"

Hallucination (S07): Give students 2 minutes to fact-check the battle date example using their phone or a textbook. Report back: "Was the AI right or wrong?"

Exit Ticket Q3: Students must name a specific verification method — not just "check the internet."
