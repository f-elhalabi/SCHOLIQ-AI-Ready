Unit 4 · Create · KC-4 AI-Enhanced Creation
How Do I Use AI to Produce Something New?
"AI can generate anything — text, image, audio, video, code, and more. But generation is not creation. AI is the tool; you are the author."
2 Sessions · 100–120 min
9 Activities
Grades 7–12

LO-1 · Define a clear creative brief before engaging any AI tool (Stage 1)
LO-2 · Engineer intentional prompts that guide AI toward a specific creative vision
LO-3 · Iterate prompts strategically and evaluate AI outputs against the original brief

LO-5 · Compose an accurate and transparent AI Disclosure Statement
LO-6 · Reflect critically on the boundary between AI contribution and personal authorship

Bridge from Unit 3In Unit 3, you learned to question and verify AI critically. In Unit 4, you apply that critical lens to your own creative process — using AI as a production partner across multiple media, without surrendering authorship. You know how to check what AI says. Now you learn how to direct what AI makes.
Why this unit?
This unit works best when teachers model creative struggle across a medium they find uncomfortable. If you always write, try generating an image. If you always use slides, try a podcast script. Students need to see that the 5-Stage Framework applies everywhere, not just in writing. Ensure every student or pair has access to both a text AI tool AND an image AI tool — these are non-negotiable for this unit.

1
The Generation Gallery
Hook
Walk through 6 AI-generated media on the same theme, then discuss who is the author.
10 min · Session 1 · Engage
Why This Activity?
Students must experience the full creative range of AI — not just text — before they can think seriously about authorship across media. The gallery format creates productive surprise without a lecture.
The Generation Gallery — same theme, 6 media
Six AI-generated examples, all built around the same theme (e.g. "the loneliness of a teenager in a big city"): 1) a short story paragraph, 2) an illustrated scene, 3) a music mood description + instrument list, 4) a podcast episode outline + intro script, 5) a social media post with caption, 6) a simple webpage concept (HTML sketch).
Complete: "I didn't know AI could ____. This changes how I think about creating because ____."
Which item surprised the most people? Why?
If AI made all six of these — who is the author?
Close the HookToday you'll learn to direct AI across any medium — and claim authorship of what you produce.
Watch For…
Students who say "all of this is fake art" — validate the concern, then redirect: "What would make it real? That's exactly what this unit answers." Students who say AI will replace artists/writers/musicians — note the tension; this becomes the ethical thread in Unit 5. Students who are immediately excited and want to generate things now — channel this energy: "You will, in 10 minutes. First, we need a framework."
2
The 5-Stage Creation Framework
Explain
Learn the 5 stages that apply to every creative medium in this unit.
8 min · Session 1 · Explain
Why This Activity?
Students need a clear, repeatable mental model BEFORE they start creating — otherwise AI becomes a shortcut rather than a co-creator. The framework applies to every medium, so students can transfer it beyond this unit.
# Stage What AI Does What the Human Does
1IdeaNothing — AI is not used yetDefines purpose, audience, medium, and creative goal
2DraftGenerates a first version based on the promptDesigns the prompt; receives and evaluates the output
3AI CollaborationRefines, varies, and expands based on follow-up promptsCompares outputs, makes selections, identifies gaps
4Human RevisionAssists on requestAdds their own knowledge, voice, aesthetic, and creative decisions
5Final Product + ReflectionUsed for Disclosure Statement claritySubmits final work with reflection and AI disclosure
Check for UnderstandingAt which stage does the human do the most work? All of them — but especially 1, 4, and 5. At which stage could a student disappear entirely and let AI do everything? Stage 2 — that is ghost generation.
Watch For…
Students who see Stage 2 as "the work" — redirect: "Stage 2 is when AI works. Stages 1, 4, and 5 are when YOU work." Students who skip Stage 1 (jump straight to prompting) — pause them: "What is your creative goal? Who is this for? Why does it matter?"
3A
Prompt Engineering Lab — Text
Elaborate
Write a Basic Prompt, then an Engineered Prompt using all 4 layers, and compare.
12 min · Session 1 · Elaborate
Why This Activity?
Students must experience prompt engineering as a skill — not a trick. The Before/After comparison makes visible how much the human's direction shapes the AI's output.
The Four-Layer Prompt Framework
Role
Defines the voice or perspective AI should take. e.g. "Write as a human rights advocate speaking to teenagers"
Task
Specifies exactly what to produce. e.g. "Write a 30-word caption for an awareness poster about screen addiction"
Constraints
Sets limits on length, tone, format, what to avoid. e.g. "Short, punchy sentences. No statistics. No hashtags."
Goal
States the emotional or creative effect to achieve. e.g. "The reader should feel a quiet unease, not guilt."
Basic Prompt (Layers 1+2 only) & AI output
Engineered Prompt (all 4 layers) & AI output
What changed? Which layer had the biggest effect? Name 3 gaps only YOUR knowledge, experience, or opinion can fill.
RememberThe prompt IS the creative decision. That decision belongs to you.
Watch For…
Students who write one-sentence engineered prompts — push: "Which of the 4 layers is missing?" Students who are satisfied with the first AI draft — challenge: "What does it NOT know about your creative goal?"
3B
Prompt Engineering Lab — Image
Elaborate
Write a Simple Image Prompt, then a Full Image Prompt using all 5 elements, and compare.
12 min · Session 1 · Elaborate
Why This Activity?
Image generation is one of the most powerful and misunderstood AI capabilities. Students who learn to engineer image prompts gain a transferable skill for design, communication, and visual storytelling — and engage with questions of authorship that are very different from text.
The Image Prompt Framework
Subject
What or who is in the image. e.g. "A teenage girl sitting alone on a city rooftop at sunset"
Style / Medium
The visual aesthetic or artistic style. e.g. "in the style of a graphic novel illustration, flat color palette"
Mood / Atmosphere
The emotional tone of the image. e.g. "melancholy, quiet, the light is warm but she looks cold"
Composition / Camera
How the image is framed. e.g. "wide shot, low angle, rule of thirds"
What to Avoid
Explicit negatives that guide AI away from unwanted elements. e.g. "no text, no people in background, no bright colors"
Simple Image Prompt (Subject only)
Full Image Prompt (all 5 elements)
What changed? Which element had the biggest impact? What creative decision did YOU make that AI could not have made without you?
Critical DiscussionIs an AI-generated image a piece of art? If you wrote the prompt, are you the artist? Never generate images that could be mistaken for real photos of real people, places, or events without clear disclosure.
Watch For…
Students who generate any image and immediately accept it — push: "Does this match what you imagined? What is wrong with it?" Students who generate inappropriate or photorealistic content — review AI tool content policies; use this as a teachable moment. Students who claim full authorship without revision — probe: "What creative decision did you make beyond typing the words?"

5
Warm-Up: Signature Marking
Engage
Mark your Activity 4 output: what's AI's, what's already yours?
8 min · Session 2 · Engage
Why This Activity?
The transition from generating to authoring requires students to notice, physically, how much of their first output is genuinely theirs. Signature Marking adapts the Voice Marking concept from text to all media — including image, audio, and multimodal work.
Track How to Mark "Yours" vs. "AI's"
A · WritingTwo pen colors: underline sentences that sound like you vs. could have been written by anyone
B · VisualTwo sticky notes: elements you specifically decided vs. elements AI chose for you
C · AudioHighlight sections: yellow = your voice; blue = generic AI language
D · MultimodalCircle the words, images, or design choices that came from your creative direction
E · CodeComment lines: // mine = logic you specified; // AI chose = generated without your direction
What percentage of this work is genuinely yours right now?
What is the most important creative decision you still need to make?
Session 2 GoalBy the end of today, the balance shifts. Your creative decisions outweigh AI's.
Watch For…
Students who mark everything as AI's — validate honesty: "That's where we start. Session 2 is where we fix it." Students who mark everything as theirs without evidence — probe: "Show me one specific decision AI couldn't have made without you." Students on Track B or D who struggle to "mark" images — guide: "Which color did you choose? Which composition? Why that mood?"
6
Revision Lab — The Human Layers
Explore & Apply
Apply the 3 Human Layers of Revision, documenting at least 5 revisions.
20 min · Session 2 · Explore
Why This Activity?
Revision is where authorship is claimed — in every medium. The Three Human Layers translate across text, image, audio, and code. Each layer addresses a different dimension of what makes the work genuinely theirs.
The Three Human Layers of Revision
Ideas / Content
Add, remove, or replace what AI got wrong, oversimplified, or missed. Writing: add facts, examples. Image: replace a misrepresenting element. Audio: add a real story. Code: fix logic errors.
Structure / Composition
Reorganize so the work flows the way YOUR thinking flows. Writing: reorder paragraphs. Image: adjust composition. Audio: move the hook earlier. Code: reorganize functions.
Voice / Signature
Rewrite, redesign, or recode at least 3 elements in your own creative style. Writing: rewrite 3 sentences. Image: add a distinctive design element. Audio: rewrite the intro in your voice. Code: rename variables to match your logic.
Revision Log — record at least 5 revisions (layer, original element, your change, why)
CoachingLayer 1 first — make sure the content is right before you worry about how it looks or sounds. Layer 3 is where your signature appears. At least 3 elements must be distinctively yours.
Watch For…
Students who only make surface changes (grammar edits, color tweaks) — push: "That's refinement, not revision. What would you change about the IDEAS?" Students using AI to revise the AI output — pause and address directly: "If AI is revising AI, where are you in this piece?" Students who feel stuck on Layer 3 — ask: "What would you do differently if AI didn't exist? That's your signature."
7
AI Disclosure Statement
Explain
Write a transparent, 6-section statement about how AI contributed to your work.
10 min · Session 2 · Explain
Why This Activity?
Transparency about AI use is not a penalty — it is an act of intellectual integrity that applies to ALL creative media. Students entering journalism, design, music, film, or software development will encounter disclosure requirements. This is future-relevant professional practice.
AI Tools Used — name every tool and its purpose
Prompts Used — basic or engineered? how many iterations? which layers?
AI Contribution — what did AI generate, get right, get wrong or miss?
Human Contribution — what did you add, change, restructure, rewrite? Be specific.
Authorship Claim — "This work is genuinely mine because ____."
My Creative Percentage — "I feel approximately ____% of this final work reflects my own creative decisions."
Model Example — Track D (Multimodal)"I used Claude for the text and DALL·E for the images in this social media campaign. I engineered prompts specifying my audience, tone, and visual style. AI produced strong initial text and image concepts — but the images were too generic. I redesigned the second image prompt entirely, rewrote all captions in my own voice, and added a call-to-action AI had not included. The campaign concept is entirely my idea. AI helped me express it."
Watch For…
Students who write vague disclosures ("AI helped me make it") — push for specificity. Students whose disclosure shows minimal human contribution — return to the Revision Log: "Five revisions minimum. Where are they?" Students who claim 100% for an output they barely revised — ask: "Would AI have produced exactly this if you had typed nothing but your name?"

The final product is the unit's summative artifact across all media. The reflection converts the product from a task completion into a genuine learning experience — students articulate what the creative process revealed about authorship, AI, and themselves as makers.
Submit your complete, human-revised artifact, your Revision Log, your AI Disclosure Statement, and a 3–5 sentence Authorship Reflection.
My Final Work (or describe & attach for Tracks B–E)
Authorship Reflection — choose at least 3 prompts, write 3–5 sentences total

"The moment I felt most like the author/designer/creator was..."

My Reflection

Students whose reflection is a summary of what they did — redirect: "Not what you did — what you discovered about yourself as a creator." Students who are proud of their work for the first time — name it explicitly: that pride is evidence of authorship.

Differentiation by Cognitive Level
All students engage with the same essential question and the 5-Stage Creation Framework. Differentiation adjusts track complexity, scaffolding depth, and range of media — not the conceptual bar.
5.1 · Grade-Band Adaptation Matrix
Grades 7–8

Produce → Revise → Label
Grades 9–10
Apply & Analyze
Compose → Layer → Justify
Grades 11–12
Evaluate & Create
Author → Interrogate → Defend Authorship
Activity 🟦 Grades 7–8 🟪 Grades 9–10 🟩 Grades 11–12
A1 · Generation Gallery Pre-annotated items with guiding questions. Complete independently; identify one item they could not have made alone. Identify one ethical concern per gallery item before discussion.
A2 · 5-Stage Framework Printed checklist, ticked off as they work. Explain the five stages in their own words first. Critique the framework: which stage carries the most human judgement, and what would automating it lose?
A3A/B · Prompt Labs Filled-in templates; blanks only for creative choices. Track A or B assigned. Write their own prompts; choose freely from Tracks A–D. 3+ iterations plus a Prompt Engineering Report comparing outputs; Track E (Code) permitted.
A4 · First Creation One creation, one medium; success = a finished artefact. A stated creative goal recorded at Stage 1, checked at the end. The creative goal includes a constraint the AI is likely to fail — anticipate the failure.
A6 · Revision Lab 3 revisions; Layers 1 and 3 only. All 5 revisions, all 3 layers, full Revision Log. Full Creator's Note justifying every revision against the Stage-1 goal.
